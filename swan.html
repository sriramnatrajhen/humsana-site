Screen a recording before you act on it.

Swan examines a voice message, a voicemail or a call recording and reports what the audio supports: the finding, the quality of the evidence behind it, and one recommended action. When a recording cannot support a finding, it says so.

01Why it is needed

Short recordings are enough to clone a voice.

Voice cloning needs only a brief sample to produce a usable imitation, and it arrives as an ordinary voice message or voicemail. Swan measures the recording and reports what the audio supports.

02What a screening returns

Three results, stated separately.

01

Authenticity

Likely human, likely synthetic, or inconclusive. Inconclusive is a normal outcome, returned whenever the recording does not support either of the other two.

02

Evidence quality

Strong, adequate, limited or insufficient. This describes the recording: its length, its bandwidth, and how much of it is speech.

03

Recommended action

Proceed, verify or hold. In the Muse connector this is the field the assistant acts on, so it is stated in the same response rather than inferred from a score.

A single score leaves its meaning to the reader. Three results cannot be read as more than they are.

03What a result means

Read the result, not a score.

It describes the recording

Not the speaker. No voiceprint is built, and no recording is compared against another.

The score is not a probability

Where the evidence is limited or insufficient, the score is withheld rather than converted into a percentage.

Ten seconds is the floor

Shorter recordings return inconclusive, with the reason stated. The errors that matter sit in the shortest clips.

Bandwidth decides

A telephone line, a compressed voice note or a noisy room lowers what the service will say. Below the noise floor it returns no determination and asks for a quieter recording. Poor audio can lower confidence. It never produces a finding on its own.

04Inside Muse

Screening where the recording arrives.

Voice messages arrive in a chat, so the screening belongs in the same place. The Swan connector is submitted to the Muse connector platform and is under review, and it is not yet listed in the directory. Once approved, a person can point at a recording in Muse and ask about it in ordinary language.

Is this voice message AI generated?Check this voicemail before I respondShould I verify the caller another way?

What comes back is a summary that reads in a chat, then the three results above. When the action is verify, the response asks the person to confirm the caller through a channel they already trust.

Swan runs as a hosted HTTPS service reached through Muse's connector framework. Nothing is required from the person using it: no account, no key and no payment. A key for your own client, or for a higher hourly limit, is issued at Get an API key, and a single recording can be screened in the browser at Screen a recording.

A socket accepts audio as it is captured, for a call in progress, and answers every few seconds while it continues. The answer may only escalate: once a window has found synthetic speech, nothing later in the session returns a cleaner verdict. Bandwidth still decides, so a wideband call can carry a finding and a telephone line is answered as inconclusive.

05Data

Recordings are not kept.

Audio is decoded in memory to produce the result and then discarded. It is not written to disk and is not used to train any model. A metadata record is kept for 30 days so a result can be explained later, and it contains no audio and no fingerprint of the audio.
