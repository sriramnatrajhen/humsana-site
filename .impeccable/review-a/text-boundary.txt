The execution boundary.

Humsana is not trying to replace the identity system, the agent runtime or the executor. It sits at the point where a consequential action crosses from one authority context into another and asks whether the authority actually covers what is being executed.

Where it does not, the action is stopped before the executor reaches the system that would perform it, and the refusal names the field, both values and the rule.

Six stages carry authority to the point of execution.

Each stage can be checked, and each can be lost. The boundary's job is the fifth one: to know what arrives at the executor and whether it is still covered.

What has to be true for this to matter.
01
The integrating party can place a check on the path that acts, on their side of the irreversible call.
02
An authority established for one system can be presented, and re-checked, in another.
03
A refusal at that seam is cheaper than reconstructing what happened afterwards.

We are testing those three with real workflows. The sandbox is the concrete part: one action class, the live service, no login, synthetic data.
