02The problem

Permission answers the wrong question.

A permission tells you what an agent can access.

It does not tell you whether this action, with these parameters, is the action someone approved.

Destination changed

The approved account is not the account in the execution request.

Artifact changed

The file, letter or batch is not the one that was approved.

Scope changed

The action reaches more people, records or resources than the approval covers.

Approval replayed

The same approval is used again.

Approval expired

The action arrives after the approval is no longer valid.

Humsana checks the action again before it happens.

03How it works

From authority to execution.

01

Claim

The agent says who it is, who it acts for, what it wants to do and what authority it has.

02

Establish

Humsana separates what is established from what is claimed and what remains unknown.

03

Confirm

The consequence of the action determines whether human confirmation is required. When it is, the approval comes from an authenticated approver.

05

Enforce

The executor presents the action immediately before it runs. If a material field has changed, Humsana refuses it and shows exactly what changed.

04The record

One record from approval to execution.

Claimed

What the agent said was true.

Established

What Humsana could actually establish.

Authorized

The exact action that was approved.

Executed

What reached the executor.

Refused

What was stopped, and why.

These states stay separate.

The approval is not rewritten after the fact. A model cannot turn a claim into evidence. A refusal stays in the record.

07The boundary

  • It is not a payment rail.
  • It is not an agent framework.

08Trust

Built for accountable automation.

Agents change who is accountable

The FCA's 2026 Mills Review discusses the move from AI assistance to AI delegation, and the resulting need for clear accountability, audit trails, explainable outcomes and meaningful human approval.

Payments already bind approval to action

Strong customer authentication requires certain remote payments to be dynamically linked to the amount and payee, with the authentication becoming invalid when those details change.

Payee changes already have a control point

Confirmation of Payee applies when a new payee is added or the details of an existing payee change.

Humsana applies the same basic principle to autonomous execution: make the approval explicit, bind it to the action, and check it again when the action happens.

We are not claiming to satisfy these regulatory requirements. We are showing that the underlying principle can be enforced for actions carried out by autonomous systems.
