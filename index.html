Where the check runs

Humsana checks the action arriving at execution against the action that was authorized, and refuses any difference. An action leaves the party that authorized it and arrives at the party that executes it, so the check sits on the executing side, immediately before the call that cannot be taken back.

Questions an engineer asks first.

What is an execution boundary?

It sits between the party that authorized an action and the party that executes it. It compares the action arriving at execution with the action that was authorized, and it decides there.

Where does it run?

Immediately before the call that cannot be taken back, on the executing side. A control inside the agent's own system cannot answer for the action once the action has left that system.

Does it replace the agent's permissions or its identity?

No. Permissions and identity settle who the agent is and what it may attempt. Humsana checks the action that actually arrives, at the moment it would take effect.

What happens when the action has changed?

The boundary names the field, both values and the rule, and refuses. The refusal is a code, the grant is not spent, and the executing call is not made.

How does somebody else check what happened?

The boundary issues a signed record of the authorization and a signed record of the execution. The verifier checks the signature, the record digests and the link between the two, offline, without an account here.

Does Humsana decide whether an action is allowed?

No. The authority is yours: your policy decides what may be authorized. Humsana checks the action against the authority that was granted, and holds or refuses when the action is not within it. The signature attests that Humsana issued the record and that it has not been altered; it does not establish that the authority behind the action was legitimate.
