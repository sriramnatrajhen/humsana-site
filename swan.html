SwanScreening for synthetic voices

Screen a recording before you act on it.

Swan examines a voice message, a voicemail, or a call recording and reports what the audio supports. Each response states the finding, the quality of the evidence behind it, and one recommended action. When a recording cannot support a finding, the response says so.

01Why it is needed

Short recordings are enough to clone a voice.

Voice cloning tools need only a brief sample of speech to produce a usable imitation. The result usually arrives as an ordinary voice message or voicemail, and the person receiving it has to decide how to respond. Swan measures the recording and reports what the audio supports.

02What a screening returns

Three results, stated separately.

Detectors commonly return a single number and leave its meaning to the reader. Swan returns three separate results, so a screening cannot be read as more than it is.

01

Authenticity

One of three values: likely human, likely synthetic, or inconclusive. Inconclusive is a normal outcome, returned whenever the recording does not support either of the other two.

02

Evidence quality

Strong, adequate, limited, or insufficient. This describes the recording itself: its length, its bandwidth, and how much of it is speech.

03

Recommended action

Proceed, verify, or hold. In the Muse connector this is the field the assistant acts on, so it is stated in the same response rather than inferred from a score.

03Limits

What the service does not claim.

These limits are enforced by the service rather than listed as disclaimers beside it.

01

No speaker identification

Swan does not report who is speaking. It does not build a voiceprint, and it does not compare a recording against any other recording.

02

No calibrated probability

The detector score is not a probability, so it is withheld when the evidence is limited or insufficient. The service does not publish a confidence percentage.

03

No finding below ten seconds

Recordings shorter than ten seconds are returned as inconclusive, with the reason stated. In our testing the errors that matter sit in the shortest clips, so the service declines to decide on them.

04

No finding from degraded audio

A telephone line, a compressed voice note, or a noisy room reduces what the service is willing to say. When speech sits too close to the background for a voice to be judged, the service returns no determination at all and asks for a quieter recording, because a clearance drawn from unreadable audio is worse than an abstention. Poor audio can lower confidence. It is never a reason for the service to report a synthetic voice, nor a reason to say a voice is human.

04Inside Muse

Screening where the recording arrives.

Voice messages arrive in a chat, so the screening belongs in the same place. The Swan connector is submitted to the Muse connector platform and is under review. It is not yet listed in the directory. Once it is approved, a person can point at a recording in Muse and ask about it in ordinary language.

01

Typical requests

  • Is this voice message AI generated?
  • Check this voice message before I respond.
  • Does this recording show signs of synthetic speech?
  • Screen this voicemail for an AI generated voice.
  • I received a suspicious call. Should I verify the caller another way?
02

What comes back

A short summary that can be read in a chat, followed by the three results above. When the recommended action is verify, the response asks the person to confirm the caller through a channel they already trust.

03

How it connects

Swan runs as a hosted HTTPS service. Muse reaches it through its connector framework. Nothing is required from the person using it: no account, no key and no payment. A key for your own client, or for a higher hourly limit, is issued at Get an API key. A single recording can also be screened in the browser, without a key: Screen a recording.

04

Audio that is still arriving

A socket accepts audio as it is captured, for a call or a recording in progress, and answers every few seconds while it continues. The answer may only escalate: once a window has found synthetic speech, nothing later in the session returns a cleaner verdict. Bandwidth still decides, so a wideband call can carry a finding and a telephone line is answered as inconclusive.

05Data

Recordings are not kept.

Audio is decoded in memory to produce the result and then discarded. It is not written to disk and is not used to train any model. A metadata record is kept for 30 days so that a result can be explained later. That record contains no audio and no fingerprint of the audio.

06Earlier engine

An open-source engine, separate from this service.

Humsana built an experimental engine that measured the physics of phonation, released under AGPL-3.0. That work is research, and the source is available on request. The hosted service described on this page does not run it. The service uses a trained detector with explicit rules for recording length and audio quality, because structured testing showed that the physics measurements did not change a decision.

A screening result is evidence, not proof.

Swan describes a recording. It does not identify the speaker, and it reports when it cannot reach a finding.
