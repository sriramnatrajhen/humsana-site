01 Authorization

When AI acts on your behalf, authority has to travel with the action.

An agent can have permission to act and still execute an action nobody approved. Humsana binds authority to the action itself, checks that action again before execution, and refuses it when it no longer matches.

Humsana is the authorization boundary for autonomous actors. The principal grants authority. Humsana records it, binds it to the action, and enforces it at the point where the action would happen.

03 The problem

Permission answers a different question from authorization.

Permission
  • Can call the payment API
  • Has a credential
  • Access persists
  • Logs what happened
Authorization
  • Can make this payment
  • Has authority
  • The grant is bounded
  • Binds what was approved
  • 01The destination changedThe approved account is not the account in the execution request.
  • 02The artifact changedThe document, filing or batch is not the one that was approved.
  • 03The scope changedThe action reaches more people, records or resources than the approval covers.
  • 04The approval was replayedThe same approval is presented a second time.
  • 05The approval expiredThe action arrives after the approval is no longer valid.

Every one of those failures happens with valid permissions.

04 The executor

The executor already has rules. They answer a different question.

The system that performs the action already decides whether the action can run.

Balance and availability

Whether the account exists and holds what the action needs.

Format and state

Whether the request is well formed and whether the record is in a state that allows the change.

Its own limits

Whether the amount or the scope is inside the ceilings the system sets for itself.

Screening and duplication

Whether the counterparty is allowed and whether this looks like a repeat.

None of them answer whether the principal authorized this action. That authority sits in a queue, a ticket, a message or a flag on a row, and it can change before the action runs.

05 The mechanism

From authority to execution.

The agent says who it is, who it acts for, what it wants to do, and what authority it has.

Humsana separates what is established from what is claimed and what remains unknown.

The consequence of the action decides whether a person has to approve it. Where one does, the approval is signed over the exact action.

The approval is tied to the action, its bound values and its expiry. It can be spent once.

The executor presents the action immediately before it runs. A material change is refused and the changed field is named.

07 Where it applies

For actions that are hard to undo.

01

Money movement

Payments, releases, beneficiary changes and payout exceptions. The account is bound at approval, so a later change is caught before the rail is called.

bound at approval → presented later → refuse
02

Regulated communication

Notices, demands and filings. The approved words and the recipient set both matter, and either can change between approval and send.

recipient set 14 → 15 → refuse
03

Destructive actions

Deleting records, revoking access, closing accounts, destroying keys. There is no second action that undoes them.

scope one account → all accounts → refuse
Any other action class the principal registers

Access, provisioning, records, commitments. The class decides which values are bound. The check is the same.

08 The boundary

Where the boundary sits.

Who authenticates the approving human

the principal's identity system
not Humsana

Humsana verifies what it is handed and records who was authenticated, by what method, and over which exact action. A production integration accepts the customer's identity, assertion or signature. The sandbox uses a reference confirmer and labels it.

Who owns the executor

the integrating party
enforced at the seam

Humsana enforces at the execution seam controlled by the integrating party. We cannot enforce an executor we do not control. The reference executor exists to demonstrate the seam and to be replaced by yours.
