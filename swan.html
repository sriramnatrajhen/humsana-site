Screen a recording before you act on it.

Swan examines a voice message, voicemail or call recording and reports what the audio supports, plus one recommended action.

Three results, stated separately.

01

Authenticity

Likely human, likely synthetic, or inconclusive. Inconclusive is a normal outcome when the recording supports neither.

02

Evidence quality

Strong, adequate, limited or insufficient: the recording's length, bandwidth and how much is speech.

03

Recommended action

Proceed, verify or hold. In the Muse connector this is the field the assistant acts on.

Read the result, not a score.

It describes the recording

Not the speaker. No voiceprint is built, and no recording is compared against another.

The score is not a probability

Where evidence is limited or insufficient, the score is withheld rather than turned into a percentage.

Ten seconds is the floor

Shorter recordings return inconclusive, with the reason stated.

Bandwidth decides

A telephone line, a compressed voice note or a noisy room lowers what the service will say. Below the noise floor it returns no determination and asks for a quieter recording. It never produces a finding on its own.

Inside Muse.

The Swan connector is submitted to the Muse connector platform Swan runs as a hosted HTTPS service, and a person using it needs no account, no key and no payment.

A socket accepts audio as it is captured and answers every few seconds. The answer may only escalate: once a window has found synthetic speech, nothing later in the session returns a cleaner verdict. A telephone line is answered as inconclusive.

Recordings are not kept.

Audio is decoded in memory and then discarded. It is not written to disk and is not used to train any model. A metadata record is kept for 30 days, holding no audio and no fingerprint of the audio.

Receives and keeps: Swan privacy policy. Terms: Swan terms of service.

A screening result is evidence, not proof. Swan describes a recording and does not identify the speaker.
