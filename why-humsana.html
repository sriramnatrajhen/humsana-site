Why Humsana exists

Why Humsana exists.

AI can already perform most of a workflow. A person is still asked to approve or release the final action, because by the time the action is about to happen it can differ from the action that was approved. Humsana sits at that point and keeps the action inside the permission that was given.

Do you need Humsana? See the boundary

The gap, in two paragraphs.

The machine can do the work. The person is kept for the last step, and usually for one reason: something consequential can change between the approval and the execution, and nobody wants that to happen unseen.

That is why a workflow that looks automated on the diagram still ends with a person pressing a button. The work moved. The responsibility for the final action did not.

Why permissions and identity do not close it.

A permission answers a question about a kind of action: may this caller do this sort of thing? An identity answers who is asking. Both are decided before the action exists in the form that will actually run, and both are usually held by the party that owns the agent.

Neither one compares the action that arrives with the action that was authorized. A permission wide enough to be useful is also wide enough to allow a changed action, and a correct identity says nothing about what that identity is about to do.

Permissions and identity remain necessary. They are simply decided earlier in the workflow than the moment where the consequence happens.

Where the check has to sit.

An action leaves the system that authorized it and arrives at the system that executes it. A control inside the first system cannot answer for the action once the action has left it. So the check sits on the executing side, immediately before the call that cannot be taken back.

Allow

The action that arrives is the action that was authorized. It proceeds.

Hold

Something consequential needs a person to confirm the exact action, and the approval is bound to it.

Refuse

The action changed. The field that moved is named, and the action does not execute.

What the customer keeps.

Two signed records: one for the authorization and one for the execution decision. Each names the key that signed it, so a third party can verify the pair without asking Humsana.

The pairing is the point. It shows what was authorized, what arrived, and what the boundary did about it, in a form that survives the workflow that produced it.

How the proof works

What changes in the workflow.

A person stops being the mandatory checkpoint for the actions the boundary can keep inside the authority that was granted. The agent carries more of the workflow, and the person is asked only where a decision is genuinely required.

Where an action should stay with a person, it stays with a person. The point is to move the work that can move and to know which work that is, which is what the assessment is for.

Where this applies.

The shape is the same wherever a machine prepares an action and a person releases it: supplier payments, production changes, shipment bookings, clinical orders, insurance payouts, purchase orders, employee changes, customer refunds and credits.

The industry changes. The point where the action becomes real does not.
