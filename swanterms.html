Who provides the service

Swan is provided by Humsana. When Swan is used inside Muse, Humsana provides the screening service and Meta provides the assistant. Meta's terms govern Muse, the account, and the conversation. These terms govern the screening and its result.

What Swan does

Swan screens a recording and reports three things separately: whether the voice is likely human or likely synthetic, how much the available evidence is worth, and a recommended action. It supports a decision about a recording. It does not replace one.

What Swan does not do

  • It does not identify a speaker. It cannot tell you who is talking, and it never reports an identity match
  • It does not report a calibrated probability. The score is a detector output, not a percentage chance, and it is withheld when the evidence is weak
  • It is not certain. Recordings of real people are sometimes flagged, and synthetic recordings are sometimes missed, particularly on telephone band audio and on short clips
  • It is not a finding about a person. It describes a recording, not anyone's intent or conduct
  • It is not a forensic instrument, and no result from it should be presented as proof in any proceeding or dispute

Minimum length and inconclusive results

Recordings shorter than ten seconds are not assessed, and the service states this rather than guessing. Longer recordings sometimes return an inconclusive result when the audio quality does not support an assessment. An inconclusive result is a normal outcome, and it means the recording should be verified another way.
