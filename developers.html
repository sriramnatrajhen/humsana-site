01Developers

Four calls, in front of the one that matters.

An extra call before the irreversible step, and a refusal your systems can act on without parsing prose.

02The shape

What your side has to do.

  • Pick one consequential action: a payment release, a beneficiary change, a bulk send, a deletion.
  • Make one extra call before it, and pass the grant with the request.
  • Keep your identity system. Humsana asks it to authenticate the approver and records the answer.
  • Add no console. The record is a receipt the boundary already holds.
03The calls

The authorization contract.

POST /v1/authorize/verify decide whether this exact action may proceed POST /v1/authorize/confirm/request ask the authenticated approver POST /v1/authorize/confirm record that approval against that instruction POST /v1/authorize/effectuate release, only if the arriving action matches the grant

A request carries who is asking, who it acts for, the action as structured fields, the authority it claims, the consequence class it declares, and when it expires. The decision returns the evidence state behind every fact, so a claim and an established fact stay apart in your logs.

authorized: false code: GRANT_DOES_NOT_COVER_THIS_ACTION matched: false consumed: false drift: <field> authorized ... arrived ... rule MAY_NOT_CHANGE

Handle a refusal as you handle a rejected instruction: do not retry it blind, and show a person what moved.

04Rules of the road

Before you write code.

A credential is capability

A key lets an agent call the boundary. It never substitutes for an approval.

Grants are single use

Do not design a retry that assumes a grant survives.

Bound sets may only shrink

Adding a recipient after approval is refused.

The provider is optional

It ships deterministic, and nothing it returns can authorize.

A refusal is a record

It is part of what you show a reviewer, not an error to find in a log later.

05Pilot
