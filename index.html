02The problem

Permission is not the same as authorization.

Permission
  • Can call the payment API
  • Has a credential
  • Access persists
  • Logs what happened
Authorization
  • Can make this payment
  • Has authority, to this account, for this amount
  • The grant is bounded, and single use
  • Binds what was approved, and compares it at execution
  • 01Destination changedThe approved account is not the account in the execution request.
  • 02Artifact changedThe file, letter or batch is not the one that was approved.
  • 03Scope changedThe action reaches more people, records or resources than the approval covers.
  • 04Approval replayedThe same approval is used again.
  • 05Approval expiredThe action arrives after the approval is no longer valid.

Humsana checks the action again before it happens.

03How it works

From authority to execution.

The agent says who it is, who it acts for, what it wants to do and what authority it has.

Humsana separates what is established from what is claimed and what remains unknown.

The consequence of the action determines whether human confirmation is required. When it is, the approval comes from an authenticated approver.

The approval is tied to the exact action, its parameters and its expiry. It can be used once.

The executor presents the action immediately before it runs. If a material field has changed, Humsana refuses it and shows exactly what changed.

07The boundary

What Humsana does not do.

  • Identity provider. Your system authenticates the approver.
  • KYC, AML or transaction monitoring.
  • Payment rail.
  • Agent framework.
  • Dashboard.
  • A model deciding what is authorized.

Humsana sits between autonomous intent and consequential execution.

08Trust

Built for accountable automation.

Agents change who is accountable

The FCA's 2026 Mills Review discusses the move from AI assistance to AI delegation, and the resulting need for clear accountability, audit trails, explainable outcomes and meaningful human approval.

Payments already bind approval to action

Strong customer authentication requires certain remote payments to be dynamically linked to the amount and payee, with the authentication becoming invalid when those details change.

Payee changes already have a control point

Confirmation of Payee applies when a new payee is added or the details of an existing payee change. Humsana carries the result of that check into the approval, and refuses a release when the result has changed.

We are not claiming to satisfy these requirements. We are showing that the principle they share, an approval bound to the exact action, is enforceable for actions no person performs.
