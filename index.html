02The problem

Permission is not the same as authorization.

A permission says: this agent can call the payment API. Authorization says: this agent can make this payment, to this account, for this amount, under this approval.

Permission systems answer whether an actor may call a function. They cannot answer whether this one instance, right now, is the instance a person approved.

Destination changed

The approved account is not the account reaching execution.

Artifact changed

An agent produces a different batch, letter or file from the one that was approved.

Scope changed

The action reaches more records, recipients or resources than the approved instruction covers.

Approval replayed

The same approval is presented a second time.

Approval expired

The approval was valid when it was granted, and the action arrives after it stopped being valid.

Humsana checks the action again at the point where it matters.

03How it works

From authority to action.

01 Claim

The agent presents who it is, who it acts for, what it wants to do, and what authority it claims. The boundary does not infer authority from the presentation.

02 Establish

Humsana separates what is established from what is merely asserted or unresolved. Measured evidence outranks an assertion, silence is recorded as silence, and unresolved is a state, not a failure.

03 Confirm

The consequence of the action decides what evidence and what human confirmation it requires. The action class decides whether a person is needed, not the caller. Where one is needed, the confirmation is asked of an authenticated approver and bound to the exact instruction.

04 Bind

The approval becomes a single-use authorization tied to the exact action, naming every field that may not change, with an expiry.

05 Enforce

The action is checked again immediately before execution. The executor presents what it is about to do, and a field that moved is refused before the action leaves, with the field, both values and the rule named in the response. What executed is recorded, and so is what was refused.

An assertion stays an assertion. Repeating it does not make it evidence.

The full mechanism
04The record

One record from decision to execution.

Claimed

What the agent presented, marked as an assertion.

Established

What Humsana could confirm, with the reason and the evidence state behind each fact.

Authorized

The binding, with every field and its digest, and the confirmation as an authenticated act with the approver and the method.

Executed

What reached the rail, and what the receipt records of it.

Refused

The action that did not leave, and why.

Execution and refusals are kept together, so the record shows what happened, what was stopped, and why.

The receipt holds these states apart. A model cannot turn a claim into evidence. An approval cannot be rewritten after the fact.

05Where it matters

Built for actions you cannot casually undo.

Money movement

Payments, beneficiary changes and payout exceptions. A payment or payout released by an agent is held until the instruction that arrives is the instruction that was approved.

Regulated communication

Approved content, recipients and policy versions must survive to execution. A notice, a demand or a filing sent by an agent, where the version and the recipient set matter as much as the words.

Destructive actions

Deleting records, revoking access, closing accounts or destroying keys. The class of action where undo does not exist.

Separate from the three above, and live today: Swan Voice, voice integrity as one evidence capability inside Humsana. It is used where a person's voice arrives as evidence.

07Boundary

What Humsana does not do.

  • Not an identity provider. The approver is authenticated by the customer's own system.
  • Not KYC or AML, and not transaction monitoring. Humsana consumes those results; it does not produce them.
  • Not a payment rail. The rail is the customer's, and it accepts an instruction only with a grant.
  • Not an agent framework.
  • Not a dashboard. There is no console to learn and no queue to manage.
  • Not a model deciding what is authorized. The decision is deterministic policy, and a model, where enabled, can only make a request stricter.
  • Not a new category. We are building the enforcement boundary, and we say what it does rather than claiming that nobody else does it.

Humsana sits between autonomous intent and consequential execution.

08Trust

Built for accountable automation.

Agentic AI and accountability

The FCA's 2026 Mills Review discusses the shift toward autonomous AI acting on people's behalf, and highlights accountability, auditability, explainability and safe deployment as considerations.

Payments already bind an action to its details

FCA strong customer authentication rules require dynamic linking of certain remote electronic payments to the amount and the payee, which includes invalidation when either changes.

Payee changes are their own control point

Confirmation of Payee is built around new payees and changes to existing payee details. Humsana holds that result inside the approved instruction rather than beside it.

Existing controls already bind parts of a consequential action. Humsana applies the same principle to autonomous execution: make the authorization explicit, bind it to the action, and check it again when the action happens.

We are not claiming to satisfy any of these requirements. We are claiming that the principle they share, an approval bound to the exact action, is buildable today for actions no person performs.
