The boundary is an extra call before an irreversible action, and a refusal your systems can act on without parsing prose.

02The shape

What your side has to do.

One action class to start

Pick one consequential action that a person cannot casually undo: a payment release, a beneficiary change, a bulk communication, a deletion. Describe its fields, and decide what has to be true before it may proceed.

One executor that can make one extra call

Anything that already calls the destination API can call the boundary first and pass the grant with the request. Nothing else in your stack has to change.

One approver route

Humsana asks your systems to authenticate a person. The approval comes back bound to the exact instruction. You keep your own identity, your own roles and your own audit trail.

No new console

There is no dashboard to learn. The record is a receipt the boundary already holds, and it can be exported.

03The calls

The authorization contract.

POST /v1/authorize/verify decide whether this exact action may proceed POST /v1/authorize/confirm/request ask the authenticated approver POST /v1/authorize/confirm record that approval against that instruction POST /v1/authorize/effectuate release, only if the arriving action matches the grant

A request carries who is asking, who it acts for, what it wants to do as structured fields, what authority it claims, the consequence class it declares, and when it expires. The decision comes back with the evidence state of every fact behind it, so a claim and an established fact are never confused in your logs.

authorized: false code: GRANT_DOES_NOT_COVER_THIS_ACTION matched: false consumed: false drift: <field> authorized ... arrived ... rule MAY_NOT_CHANGE

A refusal names the field that moved, both values and the rule it failed, as a structure. Handle it as you handle a rejected payment instruction: do not retry it blind, and show someone what moved.

04Rules of the road

Constraints worth knowing before you write code.

A credential is capability, not authorization

A key lets an agent call the boundary. It never substitutes for an approval, and it does not make an action authorized.

Grants are single use and exact-action bound

Do not design a retry loop that assumes a grant survives. It does not. A second presentation is refused, and the record shows it.

Sets may only shrink

A bound recipient set, record set or resource list may lose members and may not gain them.

The provider is optional and cannot authorize

Where a model is enabled it can only make a request stricter. The deployment runs deterministically by default, and ships that way.

Refusals are first-class

A refused action is not an error to be discovered in a log. It is recorded, and it is part of what you show a reviewer.

05Pilot

What a pilot actually is.

One action class, one executor, one approver route

We would rather do one action properly than five approximately. The first action class is chosen by the consequence, not by the ease of integration.

Your rail, your identity system

The integration is on your side of the irreversible call. We are not asking to sit in your payment path on day one, and we are not asking for production money movement during a pilot.

What you get

Authorization records for the action class you chose, refusals that name the field and the rule, and a receipt per action. Alongside them, a written statement of what is not covered.

06Not ready

What you cannot use yet.

  • No client SDK. The integration is HTTP today.
  • No self-serve key issuance. Credentials are issued by an operator against a registered organization.
  • No verification of a presented delegation. Scope, revocation and expiry for delegations are not enforced yet.
  • No high availability, no SOC 2, no ISO 27001 and no third-party audit.
  • No hosted multi-tenant service. A pilot runs on our host or yours, and we say which.

If your action class needs something on this list before it can go ahead, that is worth a conversation rather than a workaround.
