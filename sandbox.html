02What you watch

One window, and the call underneath it.

The top of the page is what a person sees at each step. Underneath, a terminal shows the real exchange between the agent, the executor and the boundary, with timestamps.

  • The hold. A payout is held because the beneficiary name does not match the account. That check is the bank's, and it already ran.
  • The repair. An agent reads the signed mandate and proposes a new destination account. Live mode is a real model call, and the claim is recorded as asserted.
  • The approval. A person approves the repaired instruction as it stands. The approval is single use, and it expires.
  • The release. The screen arrives holding the account on file, so the first press is a refusal.
  • The record. Claimed, established, authorized, refused, executed, with the digest the grant was bound to. Downloadable.
03The refusal

What it names.

refused: GRANT_DOES_NOT_COVER_THIS_ACTION field: payee_account authorized ... arrived ... rule MAY_NOT_CHANGE consumed: false

The refusal names the field, both values and the rule, as a structure. Nothing is consumed, so the approved instruction can still be sent.

  • No approval. Refused before the rail, with no grant found.
  • Past the window. Refused by the boundary's own expiry rule.
  • Replayed. Refused. A grant is spent once.
  • Straight to the rail. Refused for want of a grant. That is the integration requirement, not something the service can enforce on a rail it does not control.
04Simulated, and real

Which parts are which.

  • Simulated: the payment rail, the approval signing, and the name check. The sandbox runs the last one, standing in for the control your own systems run.
  • Real: the deterministic policy, the grants, the refusals, the receipts, and the model calls with their token counts and latency.
  • The rail: when a provider credential is present the executor calls that provider in test mode, and the provider's own record is read back at the end. Otherwise it falls back to the simulated rail and the log says so.

The sandbox is real. The authorization boundary is real. What we do not claim is on Trust.
