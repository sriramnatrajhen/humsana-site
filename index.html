Humsana makes AI agents accountable for the actions they take.

Inside your own stack, identity, policy and application controls can govern what an agent does. The hard part begins when the agent leaves it. Humsana binds authority to the action, checks that binding where the action reaches its executor, and stops execution when the action is not covered.

If a payment was authorized for account 4821 and arrives asking for 9137, it is refused before the payment system is called.

What we are looking to validate.

We are investigating one specific unanswered problem, not claiming a category. Three things we want to learn from real workflows:

  • 01Whether the execution seam can be reached in a customer's system, and who owns it.
  • 02Which action classes carry enough consequence that a person has to approve the exact action.
  • 03Whether a refusal at the seam is worth more than a detection after the fact.

The sandbox is the part that is concrete: one action class, the live service, no login, synthetic data. If the boundary is your problem, we would like to hear which action classes sit on it.
